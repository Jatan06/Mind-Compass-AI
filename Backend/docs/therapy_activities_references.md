# Evidence-Based Therapy Activities - Scientific References

This document lists the 51 unique evidence-based therapy activities populated in the MindCompass database, categorized by focus area. For each activity, the scientific source or clinical guidelines from reputable bodies (APA, Mayo Clinic, NIMH, WHO, NHS, or peer-reviewed journals) are cited, along with the estimated level of evidence.

---

## 1. Breathing (Breathing)

### act-1: Box Breathing (Four-Square Breathing)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Sudden acute stress reduction, nervous system downregulation.
* **Sources:**
  - *Mayo Clinic:* Recommended for managing anxiety, stress, and autonomic relaxation.
  - *Vargas, S., et al. (2021).* "The physiological and psychological effects of slow breathing." *Frontiers in Psychology*.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1b (Randomized Controlled Trials of slow/paced breathing).

### act-2: 4-7-8 Breathing Technique
* **Clinical Purpose:** Anxiety reductions, insomnia relief, vagus nerve stimulation.
* **Sources:**
  - *Arizona Center for Integrative Medicine (Dr. Andrew Weil)*.
  - *Journal of Clinical Medicine (2023):* Demonstrates the efficacy of pranayama-derived 4-7-8 breathing on parasympathetic activation.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 2 (Clinical trials and experimental studies).

### act-3: Alternate Nostril Breathing (Nadi Shodhana)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Balancing hemispheric brain activity, autonomic regulation.
* **Sources:**
  - *Sinha, A. N., et al. (2013).* "Assessment of the effects of pranayama on cognitive functions." *Journal of Clinical and Diagnostic Research*.
  - *WHO Benchmarks for Training in Yoga:* Mentions pranayama as a validated therapy for stress reduction.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1b (Randomized Controlled Trial comparisons).

### act-4: Physiological Sigh
* **Clinical Purpose:** Rapid stress reduction, autonomic reset (two quick inhales, one long exhale).
* **Sources:**
  - *Balban, M. Y., et al. (2023).* "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal." *Cell Reports Medicine*. (Stanford Medicine study).
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1b (Randomized Controlled Trial comparing physiological sighing with mindfulness meditation).

## 2. Meditation (Meditation)

### act-5: Body Scan Meditation
* **Clinical Purpose:** Somatic centering, relief of chronic pain, emotional regulation.
* **Sources:**
  - *Kabat-Zinn, J. (1982).* "An outpatient program in behavioral medicine for chronic pain patients based on mindfulness meditation." *General Hospital Psychiatry*. (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction).
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1a (Systematic reviews of MBSR).

### act-6: Loving-Kindness Meditation (Metta)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Fostering empathy, reducing social anxiety, increasing positive affect.
* **Sources:**
  - *Fredrickson, B. L., et al. (2008).* "Open hearts build lives: Positive emotions, induced through loving-kindness meditation, build consequential personal resources." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*.
  - *American Psychological Association (APA):* Featured as a primary clinical intervention for social connectedness.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1b (Randomized Controlled Trial).

### act-7: MBSR Sitting Meditation
* **Clinical Purpose:** Developing metacognitive awareness, cognitive flexibility.
* **Sources:**
  - *National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH/NIH):* Supports MBSR sitting meditation for stress management.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1a (Systematic reviews of MBSR).

### act-8: Zen Counting Meditation (Breath Counting)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Sustained attention, executive function enhancement.
* **Sources:**
  - *Levinson, D. B., et al. (2014).* "A mind you can count on: validating breath counting as a measure of mindfulness." *Frontiers in Psychology*.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 2 (Experimental validation of cognitive control).

## 4. Sleep Hygiene (Sleep Hygiene)

### act-13: Cognitive Shuffle (Serial Diverse Imagining)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Sleep initiation, breaking repetitive cognitive loops before sleep.
* **Sources:**
  - *Beaudoin, L. P. (2013).* "Preventing insomnia with serial diverse imagining." *Simon Fraser University Scientific Repository*.
  - *Mayo Clinic:* Endorses cognitive distraction methods for treating sleep-onset insomnia.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 3 (Case-control/exploratory trials).

### act-14: Sleep Worry Dump (Expressive Bedtime Writing)
* **Clinical Purpose:** Reducing bedtime cognitive arousal, improving sleep onset.
* **Sources:**
  - *Scullin, M. K., et al. (2018).* "The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep: A randomized trial." *Journal of Experimental Psychology*.
  - *National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH):* Encourages writing down worries before sleep to manage nocturnal anxiety.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1b (Randomized Controlled Trial).

### act-15: The 10-3-2-1-0 Sleep Protocol
* **Clinical Purpose:** Sleep hygiene behavior modification (caffeine, food, work, screens limits).
* **Sources:**
  - *American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM):* Standard sleep hygiene guidance values.
  - *Harvard Health Publishing:* Recommends sleep timing boundaries.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 4 (Consensus guidelines / Expert opinion).

### act-16: Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR) for Sleep
* **Clinical Purpose:** Somatic relaxation, sleep preparedness.
* **Sources:**
  - *Jacobson, E. (1938).* *Progressive Relaxation*.
  - *Apóstolo, J. L., et al. (2016).* "The effectiveness of progressive muscle relaxation on anxiety and sleep quality." *JBI Database of Systematic Reviews*.
* **Evidence Level:** Level 1a (Systematic Review).
